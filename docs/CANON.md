# CANON.md: Project Moon's City, a writers' reference

A canon reference for writing events, factions, careers, lore entries and quests in a fan game set in
Project Moon's City (Lobotomy Corporation, Library of Ruina, Limbus Company). Everything here is
paraphrased. Short quotes are marked as quotes.

## How to read this file

- **Source tags:** **[LobCorp]** Lobotomy Corporation (2018). **[LoR]** Library of Ruina (2020-21).
  **[Limbus]** Limbus Company (2023 onward). **[Archived]** is the Roblox fan game. It is not canon.
- **Confidence:** a plain statement comes from official game text or from several sources that agree.
  **(unverified)** means only one fan source says so, sources disagree, or it is my inference. Treat it
  as optional flavor, not fact.
- **Spoilers:** major twists are marked **[SPOILER]** so writers can skip them. The story hooks in
  section 9, and anything marked **(suggested)**, are original ideas, not canon.
- **Primary sources:** much of the Limbus material comes from the game's English text, read from a
  public dump of its localization files: Dante's in-game glossary ("Dante's Notes"), chapter titles,
  battle keyword definitions and story scripts. LobCorp Abnormality data comes from the game's
  creature files, via a public data repository. Fan wikis were checked through search, because the
  wiki sites themselves were blocked from this research session (see Sources).
- **Timeline anchor:** Limbus labels its chapters with in-game timeline codes, from 984-01 (Prologue)
  to 985-06 (the 10th Walpurgis Night). These look like year-month codes, but the calendar is never
  explained (unverified).
- **Section map:** 1 The City, 2 Districts, 3 Syndicates and the Fingers (including Ricardo), 4 Fixers
  and Associations, 5 Lobotomy Corporation, 6 Library of Ruina, 7 Limbus Company, 8 Career ladders,
  9 Story hooks, 10 Naming and surnames, 11 Archived, 12 Roblox safety, then Sources.

## 1. The City

### 1.1 Shape and rule

- **The City** is a single huge megacity made of **26 Districts**. Each District was given to a
  **Wing**, a megacorporation named after a letter. The letter's place in the alphabet is the
  District number: L Corp has District 12 and W Corp has District 23. Some slots are vacant or have
  changed hands.
- **The Head** rules everything from **District 1**. No one knows whether the Head is a person, a
  council or something else. It sets the **Taboos of the City**, which bind ordinary citizens and
  Wings alike, and it approves Wings. Fan wikis add that it manages patents and mints the currency
  (unverified).
- The Head works through three bodies, sometimes treated as one conglomerate (fan wiki):
  - **A Corp**: the Head itself. Fan sources say its Singularity is human cloning (unverified).
  - **B Corp, the Eye**: surveillance. It regulates other Wings' Singularities and watches the City
    for threats and tax dodgers. Its agents are sometimes called Beholders (unverified).
  - **C Corp, the Claw**: enforcement. Its agents, the **Claws**, carry out sentences with no appeal.
- **Arbiters** are the Head's elite agents. They are sent when a Taboo violation is serious enough.
  Fan sources say an Arbiter usually leads a group of Claws. Once the Head acts on a violation, it
  thoroughly erases everything connected to it, so that it seems never to have happened [Limbus].
- **Taboos.** **City Taboos** come from the Head and apply everywhere. **Nest Taboos** are local
  rules a Wing sets for its own Nest. Breaking a City Taboo is effectively a death sentence. A Wing
  that breaks one loses its status. Known City Taboos:
  - Bringing the dead back in body and mind (a few exceptions exist).
  - Building human-like thinking machines (the "AI Ethics Amendment").
  - Fan wikis also say only humans may live inside the City (unverified).
  - During the Night in the Backstreets: recording video or audio, and breaking into or destroying
    someone else's home [Limbus].
  - Unpaid taxes and patent infringement are punished by the Head's arms (fan wiki).
- **Singularities** are the patented, monopolized core technologies that make a company a Wing.
  Several of them visibly break physics: flesh regrowing from a severed arm, stickers that raise
  your luck. A Wing can own more than one. A Wing pays for its Nest with Singularity income and very
  high taxes.
- **Feathers** is the nickname for people who work for a Wing.
- **Currency: Ahn.** Canon rarely gives prices. Story text mentions debts in the tens of millions,
  tax bills of a few hundred thousand, and a T Corp "Time Tax" bill over ten billion. Writers can set
  their own scale.

### 1.2 Nests, Backstreets, Outskirts

- **Nest:** the walled, high-class part of a District. The Wing manages and protects it. Getting in
  involves checkpoints and paperwork, and the border with the Backstreets is sharply drawn. Each Nest
  has its own culture and government: S Corp has a King with retainers, H Corp a Family Hierarch,
  and T Corp a Chief Executive Director.
- **Backstreets:** everything in the District outside the Nest. It is home to people who cannot
  afford Nest residence. The streets are packed with restaurants, small businesses, housing, schools
  and academies. They are also twisty, damp and dangerous, with Syndicates, Fixers and the nightly
  Sweepers.
- **Travel:** roads between Backstreets areas are so labyrinthine that a short trip by car can take
  very long. Highways link the Nests but come with conditions and fees. W Corp's **WARP trains**
  cross the City in seconds, but tickets are expensive [Limbus].
- **Compass regions:** Fixers, and especially Association branches, differ by cardinal region
  (North, South, East, West) in uniform and custom. Working in the region where your Association has
  its headquarters carries prestige, and a transfer between regions can be a promotion [Limbus].
- **The Outskirts:** the zone outside the Nest areas and the City proper. It holds **monsters**
  that are neither Abnormalities nor Distortions and that hate humans. It also has human villages,
  factories, and intelligent non-humans called Gnomes and Giants. The **Great Lake**, a huge
  patchwork lake at the City's south end, overlaps the Outskirts and is full of storms and monsters
  [Limbus].

### 1.3 Strange things: a working taxonomy

- **Abnormalities:** impossible beings with extraordinary powers that fit no human or animal mold.
  Faust insists they are a category of their own. When defeated they revert to an egg-like form
  instead of dying for good. L Corp graded them with five risk levels (section 5). [SPOILER:
  LobCorp's story ties their origin to humanity's collective psyche, "the Well".]
- **Distortions:** people who turn into monstrous forms when pushed to an emotional extreme. Not
  everyone at the brink Distorts, so a second trigger probably exists. They can be killed for good.
  Limbus research describes stages, like a disease: Incubation, Early, Middle and Late. There are
  also **Location-type Distortions** that warp a whole street. A Distortion's power can rival a
  Singularity.
- **Peccatula:** beast-like things that resemble Distortions. They are probably what fails to
  become a Distortion. They grow through stages 1 to 3 [Limbus].
- **Monsters:** non-Abnormal creatures of the Outskirts and the Great Lake, such as Trash Crabs.
- **Bloodfiends:** red-eyed, regenerating people who need fresh blood and form "Families" of
  kindred. They are not Distortions [Limbus, Canto VII].
- **Shin (心) and Mang (望):** a trainable inner power that strengthens the body through emotion and
  meditation. It shows as a golden aura. Mang condenses it into rings of light around limbs or
  weapons. Each extra ring roughly doubles the mental strain. Someone who has never reflected on
  their sins reportedly cannot use it [Limbus, recent].

### 1.4 Eras and events (oldest first)

1. **The Wings' order.** The Head, the Wings and the Taboos are ancient as far as anyone knows. Their
   origin is never explained.
2. **The Smoke War.** A war between Wings, with the **Old L Corp** (an L Corp that existed before
   Lobotomy Corporation) as a main party. The **Old G Corp** of District 7 fought on the front lines
   with insect-themed "decisive weapons": rumored names include the Maggot Prince, the Moth Princess
   and the Lord of Flies, plus the urban-legend **Roach Emperor**. Old G Corp collapsed afterward and
   a new G Corp took the slot (fan wiki). Simply having a record of fighting in the war can bar you
   from benefits such as Nest residence, whichever side you fought on. Veterans rarely talk about
   it.
3. **Lobotomy Corporation's rise.** The new L Corp extracts **Enkephalin**, a glowing green fuel,
   from contained Abnormalities and powers much of the City.
4. **The White Nights and Dark Days.** At the end of LobCorp's story a pillar of light bursts from L
   Corp's headquarters and spreads across the City. Accounts of the days differ. The Limbus wiki
   describes about a week, with three good days before the light dispersed. The LoR wiki describes
   three days of light and then a struggle (unverified). L Corp collapses, its branch facilities shut
   down and are buried, its Wing slot stays empty, and District 12 falls into disorder. Almost
   everyone in the City remembers this event.
5. **The Library.** A tower, the Library, appears where L Corp's headquarters stood. It invites
   guests who are turned into books if they lose. It later vanishes [LoR].
6. **The Distortion Phenomenon.** It began after the White Nights and Dark Days, set off by the
   light. It continues even after the Library returns most of the Light to the City at the end of
   LoR. Fan wikis tie the ongoing Distortions to Carmen [SPOILER] (unverified).
7. **The Limbus era.** Limbus Company searches buried L Corp branches for **Golden Boughs** and sells
   Distortion management. Its bus visits Districts 4, 10, 11, 21, 20, 16, 8 and 14.
8. **Possible futures.** A future version of a Sinner reports a **Second Smoke War** in his world.
   This is a possibility, not an established fact [Limbus].

### 1.5 The Night in the Backstreets and the Sweepers

- Every night at **3:13 AM** the **Sweepers** rise out of the ground along every Nest's border and
  march through the Backstreets for **80 minutes**. They come in **three waves** with roughly ten
  minutes of quiet in between [Limbus, Dante's Notes]. Some fan sources give slightly different
  times (unverified).
- They dissolve and remove whatever needs cleaning: dead bodies, unauthorized construction, and
  anyone caught outdoors. They can even consume their own to heal. Faust says the Head wields them.
- During the Night it is forbidden to record video or audio and to break into or destroy someone
  else's home. In practice that makes a locked home a sanctuary.
- In LobCorp the Sweepers appear as the Indigo Ordeal. LoR has a Sweepers reception, and Limbus has
  them in "Nocturnal Sweeping".
- Everyday rhythm: be indoors well before 3 AM, and never be the person still haggling at a stall
  when the clock turns.

### 1.6 Rats and ordinary life

- **Rats** are the bottom rung of Backstreets crime: small-time thugs and muggers with no Syndicate
  backing. LoR has them among its earliest, weakest guests (exact order unverified). A Limbus line
  dismisses a group as "just another pack of dirty Rats living in the Backstreets". Exactly what
  defines a "Rat" beyond being low-level, unaffiliated Backstreets crooks is not spelled out in the
  text I could read (unverified).
- **Money rules everything.** Fixers do not work for free, protection is paid for, and better bodies
  are bought. Prosthetics and augmentation are common; red combat-augmented eyes are a frequent
  sight. Several regeneration technologies exist, such as K Corp's ampules and life-insurance
  revival services, and most of them need the brain intact.
- **Backstreets survival rules:** know the Fingers' rules, pay the local Syndicate, hire Fixers for
  anything dangerous, keep off the streets during the Night, and do not anger anyone with a book of
  grudges.
- **Nest life:** safer and richer, but watched, taxed and bound by both the Wing's local rules and
  the Taboos. Many Nests treat luxuries strangely: color is a privilege in T Corp, and the size of
  your room tracks your net worth in H Corp.
- **Common jobs** (more in section 8): shop and restaurant work, schools and academies, Wing
  "Feather" jobs, Fixer work (very common), Butlers (long-contract Fixers), couriers, cleanup crews,
  tax collectors, and miners.

## 3. Syndicates and the Five Fingers

"Syndicate" is the City's word for any gang or organization with a name. The **Five Fingers** are
the five largest and most powerful Backstreets Syndicates. Even Wings and Associations avoid
picking fights with them, and Backstreets people learn their rules to survive. Each Finger is built
around a virtue. The pattern matches the five Confucian constants (仁義禮智信: benevolence,
righteousness, propriety, wisdom, trust). Only the Pinky's character (仁) is stated outright; the
others are my inference:

| Finger | Virtue | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| **Thumb** | Propriety (禮) | Rank and etiquette above all |
| **Index** | Trust or faith (信) | Obeys written Prescripts without question |
| **Middle** | Loyalty or righteousness (義) | A family that never forgets a grudge |
| **Ring** | Wisdom or knowledge (智) | Art and knowledge, pursued to grotesque extremes |
| **Pinky** | Humanity (仁) | Anonymous members hidden inside other groups |

- The Fingers have regional branches (for example "the Thumb East"), and their dress varies less by
  region than Association uniforms do.
- **The House of Spiders [Limbus, SPOILER-light]:** a rare joint project of all five Fingers. Each
  Finger sent a **Nursefather** and an **Apprentice** to train one person with an aptitude for a
  legendary blade into something like a living Concept Incinerator. That person became Ryōshū.
- A **Finger Bow-Bell** is a summit of the Fingers. Its details are unknown.

### 3.1 The Thumb

- **Style:** obsessed with propriety. Status ("rank" or "class") is defined by very detailed rules.
  Members are expected to show deference upward, and they punish disrespect violently, sometimes
  over a single word. In Limbus, just saying a Capo's name without proper respect earns a threat of
  maiming. The rules are much the same in every branch. Their fighters are known for firearms, and
  Limbus's "Ammo" resource seems to belong mainly to Thumb identities (unverified).
- **Ranks**, bottom to top (exact sub-ranks per fan wiki; Capo IIII and Sottocapo confirmed by game
  text):
  1. **Soldato** (soldier), graded **Primo, Secondo, Terzo, Quarto**. Limbus has a "Thumb East
     Soldato II".
  2. **Capo** (captain), graded **I to IIII**. **Capo IIII** is the highest Capo grade, directly
     below a Sottocapo.
  3. **Sottocapo** (underboss): runs the Syndicate's operations in a designated area.
  4. **Capo dei capi** (boss of bosses): the top (fan wiki).
- **Notable members:**
  - **Lei Heng**, Thumb East Capo IIII [Limbus, Canto VIII]. Brutish, feared even by high-ranking
    people. The LCB rates him more powerful and violent than Ricardo.
  - **Valencina**, the Thumb's Nursefather in the House of Spiders, and **Lucio**, its Apprentice
    [Limbus].
  - In LoR: **Sottocapo Kalo** and the Capos **Boris, Denis and Katriel** (fan wiki).

### 3.2 The Index

- **Style:** unwavering faith in **Prescripts**, orders that members follow without question.
  Ordinary members receive them on paper slips brought by **Messengers**; senior members may receive
  them on a device. Who writes the Prescripts is unknown.
- **Roles:** **Proselytes** train under a **Proxy** and can later rise to **Proxy** or **Messenger**
  (fan wiki). **Messengers** receive and deliver Prescripts. The exact hierarchy is unknown even in
  game text. One fan source also mentions "Weavers" (unverified).
- **Notable:** **Rien**, the Index Nursefather, and **Sora**, the Index Apprentice [Limbus]. In LoR,
  the Index Proxies, including a Messenger named Yan (fan wiki).
- **Writing note:** Prescripts make a great engine for odd, safe events if they are absurdly specific,
  for example (an invented example, not canon text): at 4:12, buy a red umbrella and give it to the
  third stranger you pass.

### 3.3 The Middle

- **Style:** loyalty and family. Members call each other **Little Brother/Little Sister** and **Big
  Brother/Big Sister** and treat each other as real family. Their tattoos identify them on sight.
  They write grudges in a **Book of Vengeance** and pursue anyone written in it. Their creed, in
  paraphrase: the Middle remembers, and it always pays its debts in some form. Their revenge can
  fall on a whole neighborhood [SPOILER-light: one such reprisal haunts Rodion's past].
- **Ranks:** Little Brother/Sister, then Big Brother/Sister. Ricardo mentions a "**Great Sister**"
  he would consult, which suggests a higher rank (unverified). The House of Spiders had a Middle
  Nursefather, **Matthias**, and Apprentice, **Kira**.
- The Project Moon comic *Leviathan* reportedly shows the Middle and the Ring in more depth
  (unverified).

### 3.4 Ricardo (exact canon identity)

The owner asked for him by name, so here is the precise record:

- **Who:** **Ricardo**, a **Big Brother of the Middle**, one of the Five Fingers. **He is not a
  member of the Thumb.** No Ricardo turned up in LobCorp or LoR.
- **Where:** **Limbus Company, Canto V, "The Evil Defining"**: U Corp, District 21, on the Great Lake.
  He is a boss of that Canto. He returns in the Intervallo **"Nocturnal Sweeping"** (H Corp,
  District 8), where a Middle Little Brother named Werner has guided him through Hongyuan. Later
  chapters use him as a yardstick for how dangerous a Finger can be: in Canto VIII Dante worries
  about ending up with "another Ricardo" hunting the bus.
- **Look:** sunglasses even in gloomy weather, Middle tattoos, and a heavy old book (his Book of
  Vengeance) chained at his waist.
- **Personality:**
  - Supremely confident and imperious.
  - Playful and cruel in a fight: he laughs and claps while the Sinners keep reviving, and suggests
    "clock bashing" Dante.
  - Turns grand and archaic when he reads out someone's "sins" from the Book.
  - Vain about his hair: he lectures on the "luscious" and the "bristly" world.
  - Petty about what sparks a feud, deadly once one is going, and he never forgets.
- **His grudge:** someone raided his personal safe. Among the loot was a coupon for five hours of
  personal hair care from the director of a top luxury salon on U Corp's Stingray Portship.
  Heathcliff had taken things without knowing whose they were. The LCB assumed the trouble was about
  their scuffle with the Twinhook Pirates' first mate, Ms. Smee, but Ricardo had never heard of her.
- **Outcome:** the **Indigo Elder**, a Color Fixer and old harpooner famous for hunting the Marlin
  Whale, wounds him badly with a thrown harpoon. Ricardo withdraws, warning that the Middle remembers.
  In "Nocturnal Sweeping" he ends up enduring the Sweepers' waves near the LCB. A shifting building
  launches him into the skyline while he swears this is "also going in the books".
- **Writing him safely:** keep the menace and the comedy (hair vanity, petty triggers, theatrical
  sentencing) and keep the violence offscreen.

### 3.5 The Ring

- **Style:** devoted to art and knowledge. Canon Ring "art" is grotesque and made from people. For
  Roblox, reframe it as eerie avant-garde art (see section 12).
- **Structure:** **schools** or movements with their own aesthetics: **Pointillists**,
  **Fauvists** (with schisms of their own) and **Corporists** (who field "Automanikins").
  - Ranks seen: **Student**, then **Docent**.
  - A **Maestro** is acknowledged for founding a movement. The Fauvist Docent Rufo says Ryōshū is on
    the way to founding her own.
  - Schools keep **galleries**. The Ring keeps coming up in connection with the mysterious Corridor.
- **Notable:** **Callisto**, the Ring Nursefather, and **Albina**, the Ring Apprentice [Limbus].
- During the LoR era the Ring fought a war with the Pinky in the Outskirts (fan wiki).

### 3.6 The Pinky

- **Style:** follows the Virtue of Humanity (仁), and is the most cryptic Finger. Members do not
  wear uniforms. They live anonymous lives inside other organizations, even other Fingers, until
  they are summoned together under certain conditions.
- **Notable:** **Shiomi Yoru**, the Pinky Nursefather, and **Ren**, the Pinky Apprentice [Limbus].

## 4. Fixers, Associations, Offices and Workshops

### 4.1 Fixers

- **What they are:** licensed freelancers for hire. They take escort, retrieval, protection,
  investigation and combat jobs, and assassination is in high demand. It is one of the City's most
  common careers, and its people come from every walk of life.
- **Licensing:** you must pass tests to get a license, and the **Hana Association** issues them.
  Every Fixer must belong to an authorized organization, an **Office** or an **Association**. A
  one-person Office is allowed.
- **Grades:** **Grade 9** is the lowest and **Grade 1** the highest. Fan sources say Hana promotes
  and demotes based on the reports Fixers file after jobs, and can demote for unprofessional
  conduct.
- **Color Fixers:** a special status above the scale, granted by Hana. Each is known by a color and
  an epithet, and Don Quixote calls them "every Fixer's dream". Known Colors:
  - The **Red Mist** (Kali) [LobCorp, LoR].
  - The **Black Silence** (Roland) [LoR].
  - The **Blue Reverberation** (Argalia) [LoR].
  - The **Purple Tear** (Iori), who is searching for her missing son (fan wiki) [LoR, Limbus].
  - The **Red Gaze** (Vergilius) [Limbus].
  - The **Indigo Elder** and the **Yellow Harpoon** [Limbus].

## 5. Lobotomy Corporation [LobCorp]

### 5.1 What L Corp was

- **Lobotomy Corporation** (L Corp) was the Wing of **District 12** and an **energy company**.
  Abnormalities were brought in as "eggs", held in **Containment Units** and worked with every day.
  The work made them release **Enkephalin**, a glowing green energy that powered much of the City,
  including other Wings.
- L Corp had **branch facilities** across the City. After the company fell they shut down and were
  buried. Their ruins are where Limbus Company digs for Golden Boughs.
- Enkephalin is also a substance with mind-altering effects. A Limbus warning label notes it is
  hallucinogenic if swallowed. For Roblox, present it only as fuel (section 12).
- **E.G.O** ("Extermination of Geometrical Organ", a garbled acronym even in canon) is weapons and
  armor extracted from an Abnormality. It is made from that Abnormality's unique PE-Boxes and carries
  the Abnormality's character, which can change the wearer's mood or speech.

### 5.2 How a workday ran

- The **Manager** sends **Agents** into Containment Units to do one of four kinds of work.
  - A good result yields **PE-Boxes** (Positive Enkephalin, green).
  - A bad result releases **Negative Enkephalin** and hurts the Agent.
  - Each Abnormality has favored and disliked work types, and the facility unlocks its details
    (its encyclopedia) by gathering observations.
- **Qliphoth Counter:** each Abnormality's stability. It drops when the Abnormality is displeased.
  At zero, the Abnormality **breaches** (escapes) or triggers a special ability, and Agents must
  **suppress** it.
- **Qliphoth Meltdown:** every work session fills a facility-wide gauge. When it is full,
  containment alarms go off across several units. Sometimes an **Ordeal** arrives instead (5.6).
- **LOB points** pay for hiring and for raising a new Agent's Virtues. Fear damage is worse when a
  low-level Agent faces a high-risk Abnormality.

### 5.3 Risk levels and work types

- **Risk levels**, lowest to highest: **ZAYIN, TETH, HE, WAW, ALEPH**. L Corp ranked Abnormalities
  by how efficiently they produced energy. The in-game manual simply calls ALEPH the most dangerous.
  In the Limbus era, Limbus Company re-grades entities with an added "expected damage" number, so an
  "HE-5" can be more dangerous in a fight than a "WAW-4".
- **Work types.** Each raises one Virtue at the end of the day:

| Work | What it is (own words) | Raises | Damage color |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instinct** | Meeting the Abnormality's physical needs and urges | **Fortitude** (HP) | Red (physical) |
| **Insight** | Keeping its unit and living conditions in good order | **Prudence** (SP, sanity) | White (mental) |
| **Attachment** | Emotional contact: talking, play, companionship (unverified wording) | **Temperance** (work success and speed) | Black (both) |
| **Repression** | Force and discipline to keep it in check | **Justice** (attack and movement speed) | Pale (cuts a share of total health) |

- The game data uses R, W, B and P (Red, White, Black, Pale) as codes for both the work types and
  the damage types. The Attachment and Repression stat links are well known but I did not see them
  stated in the tutorial text (unverified).

### 5.4 Staff, Virtues and the four virtues as stats

- **Agents:** employees who work with and fight Abnormalities. They start at **Level 1** and can be
  promoted to **Level 5** as their Virtues rise. Each Virtue has its own level, and higher-level
  Agents fear less.
- **Clerks:** non-combat office staff in each department. As long as enough of them survive, the
  department gets a bonus ("Clerk Benefits"). They panic easily when left without Agents.
- **Continued Service:** Agents who stay in one department earn a title and an armband. The
  longest-serving Agent with more than 7 days becomes the department's **Captain**, with a unique
  armband. Transferring out loses it.
- **Agent titles** combine a personality word ("Timid", "Steadfast", "Cold-Blooded") with a role or
  merit word ("Researcher", "Veteran", "Old-Timer", "Specialist", "Senior", "Slayer", "Investigator",
  "Philosopher", "Commander"). Useful flavor for in-game titles.
- **The four virtues:** **Fortitude** (endurance, HP), **Prudence** (mental resilience, SP),
  **Temperance** (patience and skill: work success and speed), **Justice** (decisiveness: attack
  and movement speed). The kit's existing stats map onto these directly.

### 5.7 Story at a glance [SPOILERS throughout]

1. **The lab:** years earlier, the researcher **Carmen** gathered colleagues, friends and former
   patients into a research group hidden in the Backstreets. They hoped to cure the "sickness" of
   human minds. Its people included **Ayin** and those who later became the Sephirot (5.5),
   protected by the Fixer **Kali, the Red Mist**. Exactly who joined when is partly unverified.
2. **The fall of the lab:** a betrayal led the Eye to their hideout, and the Head sent Claws. The
   group captured an enemy agent, **Garion**, alive. Many members died over the course of the
   research. **Carmen died too**; keep that offscreen (section 12).
3. **L Corp:** Ayin turned the work into **Lobotomy Corporation**, which ran on Abnormalities. He
   built the Sephirot from his dead colleagues' minds and built **Angela**, modeled on Carmen.
4. **The loop:** the player, **X**, manages the facility through repeated days and resets while
   recovering lost memories. The goal is the **Seed of Light**, a light meant to awaken and heal
   humanity's hearts.
5. **The ending:** the Light is released (the **White Nights and Dark Days**). Angela, worn down by
   an immense time trapped in loops, breaks from the plan and takes much of the Light to build the
   **Library** [LoR].

### 5.8 After L Corp

- L Corp collapsed and its Wing slot is still empty. Its branches were buried, its District fell
  into disorder, and its technology scattered: E.G.O gear, Enkephalin modules and boxes still carry
  the old logo.
- Limbus Company later uses the same risk levels and studies Abnormalities, Distortions and
  Peccatula in its Enkephalin department (the LCE). The LCE's interiors resemble the old L Corp.

### 6.2 Invitations, Receptions and books

  the Librarians in a battle called a **Reception**.

  - A guest who **loses** is turned into a **book**. In canon the Library never loses.
- Defeated guests' books are "burned" to unlock **Key Pages** (a person's body, stats and
  resistances, worn by Librarians) and **Combat Pages** (techniques). Combat Pages cost **Light**.
  **Emotion Level** rises as Librarians win or lose clashes and roll their best or worst, and it
  refreshes their Light.
- Each Floor also faces **Abnormality pages** from its old LobCorp department. Each Patron Librarian
  goes through a **Floor Realization**, confronting their LobCorp past and gaining E.G.O pages.
- Guests are grouped by Hana's hazard grades, which double as story chapters: Canard, Urban Myth,
  Urban Legend, Urban Plague, Urban Nightmare, Star of the City, Impuritas Civitatis.

### 6.3 Roland, the Black Silence

### 7.5 Status effects: exact mechanics

Every status effect has **Potency** (strength) and **Count** (how many more times it works). The
rules below are paraphrased from the game's own keyword definitions:

| Effect | Trigger and effect | Count goes down |
|---|---|---|
| **Burn** | At **turn end**, take fixed damage equal to Potency | By 1 after each trigger |
| **Bleed** | When the unit **tosses an attack Coin** (attacks), it takes fixed damage equal to Potency | By 1 after each trigger |
| **Tremor** | Does nothing alone. When hit by a skill that causes **Tremor Burst**, the unit's **Stagger Threshold rises** by Tremor Potency | By 1 at turn end |
| **Rupture** | When **hit** by an attack, take fixed damage equal to Potency | By 1 after each trigger |
| **Sinking** | When **hit**, lose SP (Sanity) equal to Potency. Units without SP take Gloom damage instead | By 1 after each trigger |
| **Poise** | When hitting, a Potency-based chance to land a **Critical Hit**. Wiki figures: 5% per Potency, crits deal +20% damage | By 1 when a crit lands, and by 1 at turn end |
| **Charge** | A **resource** that certain skills spend for extra effects. Count caps at 20 | By 1 at the end of each turn. Some newer W Corp-style kits also refer to "Charge Potency" (unverified) |

- **Tremor Burst** is a skill effect, not a status. It applies the target's Tremor right away:
  their Stagger Threshold rises by their Tremor Potency. The threshold is the yellow line on the HP
  bar. When HP falls below it, the unit is **Staggered**: it cannot act and its physical resistances
  become "Fatal". Raising the line can stagger a target without more damage. Passing several lines in
  one turn raises the **Stagger Level** (up to 3), which increases damage taken. Newer variants
  include Tremor - Chain and Tremor - Fracture.
- **Sinking Deluge** (a skill effect): deals SP damage equal to Sinking Count times Potency, then
  removes Sinking. SP loss past -45 becomes Gloom damage to HP.
- **Other common keywords:**
  - **Haste / Bind:** Speed up or down by their Count for one turn.
  - **Fragile / Protection:** take more or less damage, about 10% per Count, up to 10.
  - **Damage Up; Offense Level Up / Defense Level Up.**
  - **Paralysis:** zeroes some coins' power (unverified wording).
  - **Ammo:** spent by some skills; some attacks fail without it (mostly Thumb identities;
    unverified).
  - **Nails:** a unique Bleed used by N Corp.

### 7.7 How a clash works

1. Each skill has a **Base Power**, a number of **Coins** and a **Coin Power**, which can be plus or
   minus.
2. **Final Power = Base Power + (Coins that land Heads × Coin Power).**
3. **Heads chance = 50% + current Sanity (SP).** SP runs from -45 to +45, so heads chance runs from
   5% to 95%. Units with no SP flip at 50%.
4. In a **Clash**, both skills flip and compare power. The loser loses one Coin. They repeat until one
   side runs out of Coins. The winner then attacks with its remaining Coins, one hit per Coin, and
   gets a damage bonus that grows with the number of clashes.
5. **Sanity** goes up when you win clashes and kill enemies, and down when you lose clashes or an
   ally dies. Low SP brings **Low Morale** and then **Panic**. Using E.G.O at low SP risks
   **Corrosion**, which is guaranteed at -45: the corroded Sinner attacks friend and foe, recovers
   the next turn, and resets to 0 SP.
6. **Speed** is rolled each turn. A faster unit can redirect an enemy's attack onto itself and force
   a clash. A slower or equally fast unit trades unopposed hits instead.
7. **Defense skills:**
   - **Guard** reduces damage.
   - **Evade** dodges completely if its power matches or beats the attack.
   - **Counter** lets the enemy hit first, then strikes back unopposed.
8. Every 3 points of **Offense Level** advantage add +1 Clash Power.
9. **Damage types:** Slash, Pierce and Blunt. Resistance labels, worst to best for the defender:
   Fatal, Weak, Normal, Endure, Ineffective, Immune.

## 8. Career ladders

consistent sources. **(suggested)** rows are my game-design proposals that fit canon but are not
canon. **(unverified)** marks shaky canon.

### 8.1 Patterns of advancement the City actually uses

These are good mechanics to reuse:

- **Exams and licenses:** Fixers must pass Hana's tests for a license and are graded from job
  reports. Hana can promote or demote.
- **Records decide promotion:** at L Corp the Record Team's records and assessments decide contracts
  and promotions, so staff try to stay on its good side.
- **Seniority perks:** L Corp gives Continued Service titles and armbands, and a department Captain
  armband to the longest-serving Agent once they pass 7 days. Transferring departments resets this.
- **Numbered classes:** Wing staff hold "Classes". In K Corp's Nest, **Class 1 is the lowest** and
  bigger numbers are better. T Corp has a Class 5 Audit Staff whom others treat as very senior. A
  joke in canon: "it depends on the Wing", so some Wing could have Class 12 or 20.
- **Performance gates:** W Corp lets only Level 4+ Cleanup Agents with top performance wear the CCA
  power armor.
- **Sections:** in Associations, lower Section numbers are more senior. A Liu South Fixer describes
  being stuck in Section 4 and finally rising to Section 3. A Director leads each Section. Working in
  the HQ region is prestigious, and regional transfers can be promotions.
- **Etiquette and deference:** the Thumb promotes within a rigid hierarchy and punishes disrespect.
- **Money as status:** in H Corp your room grows or shrinks with your net worth. In T Corp,
  time-wealth decides how long your day is. Nest residence must be paid for, and Smoke War veterans
  can be refused it.

## 9. Story hooks (spoiler-light seeds for multi-year arcs)

These are original seeds that fit canon. None of them is a canon event. Each can run as a chain:
an opening event, a follow-up scheduled years later, and a payoff. Arc shapes are suggestions.

### 9.1 By era

**Smoke War aftermath (a grandparent's generation):**
- **The barred veteran:** Grandpa is refused Nest residence because of an old war record. *Arc:*
  find proof he served on the "right" side, discover there is no right side, then choose between

## 10. Naming style and original surnames

### 10.1 How names look in the City

- **Mixed origins:** Korean (Dongrang, Samjo, Jae-heon, Yujin), Japanese (Ryōshū, Iori, Shiomi
  Yoru), Chinese (Hong Lu, Jia Xichun, Lei Heng, Xiao), German (Hermann, Werner, Kromer,
  Siegfried), Italian and Spanish (Valencina, Lucio, Ricardo, Rufo), French (Meursault, Olivier,
  Pierre), Russian (Rodion, Sonya, Nikolai, Maxim, Tanya) and English (Heathcliff, Catherine, Jack)
  all sit side by side. Nobody remarks on it.
- **Single names are normal.** Most people go by one name. Surnames show up mainly for old families
  (the Jia family, the Edgar family) or literary references (Sinclair, Meursault).
- **Titles before names:** rank or office comes first, as in Capo IIII Lei Heng, Big Brother
  Ricardo, Director Yujin, Team Leader Moses, Chief Researcher Hohenheim, Chief Butler.
- **Epithets:** descriptive nicknames such as Bamboo-hatted Kim, the Indigo Elder, the Puppeteer,
  the Human Thunderbolt. **Color titles** follow the pattern color + evocative noun: Red Mist, Black
  Silence, Blue Reverberation, Purple Tear, Red Gaze, Yellow Harpoon.
- **Group naming conventions:** some Syndicates name members by a shared theme. TV Tropes notes one
  whose members are all named after animal sounds (unverified which group).
- **Places and groups:** Offices are often one sharp English noun or the founder's name (Hook,
  Wedge, Dawn, Full-Stop, MultiCrack, Fanghunt, Firefist, Molar, Streetlight, Gaze, Cane, Yun's,
  Jeong's). Workshops sound like brands (Old Boys, Mook, Allas, Zelkova, Ranga, Rosespanner, Wheels
  Industry, Crystal Atelier, Atelier Logic). Abnormalities have fairy-tale or poetic names.
- **Kit tip:** East Asian names in canon often put the family name first (Jia Xichun). With the
  kit's `{first} {last}` tokens, either store such names Western-style or add a name-order flag.

## 11. Archived (the Roblox fan game): ideas worth borrowing

Use these as **ideas, not assets**. Details come from third-party guides and a fan wiki and change
between updates, so treat the numbers as approximate.

### 11.1 What it is

- **Archived** is a Roblox action RPG by the developer **Darillu**, directly inspired by LobCorp,
  LoR and Limbus. The developers keep an official Trello board (mechanics, weapons, skills,
  locations, dungeons, lore). There is also a Discord and a BloxInformer wiki. Guides in 2025
  describe it as pre-release or private at times; check its live status. Codes are redeemed with
  `/redeemcode` in chat.

### 11.2 Core loop

1. Explore the **Backstreets**, take **contracts** and **quests**, fight enemies.
2. Earn **XP** and **Ahn**, then pass **Grade-up exams** to climb from Grade 9 to Grade 1.
3. Run **dungeons**. The flagship, **the Library**, is entered by **invitation** with up to 8
   players.
4. **Craft** and buy gear. Recipes are named after canon Workshops: Allas Workshop, Atelier Logic,
   Crystal Atelier, Durandal, Mook Workshop, Old Boys Workshop, Wheels Industry, Zelkova Workshop.
5. Late game: **manifest E.G.O**.

### 11.3 Systems

- **Character creation by dialogue.** You pick "Ponder" and answer a branching conversation that
  decides your starting **affiliation**. Guides map study-related choices to the Zwei, Liu, Shi and
  Seven Associations, and the Thumb Syndicate is also possible. Skipping creation gives no
  affiliation and a Greatsword.
- **Associations vs Syndicates.** Associations are the official Fixer path; Syndicates are the
  outlaw path. Guides disagree on whether you can switch later. One says a Syndicate member can
  become a Fixer but not the reverse; another says Syndicates limit your Fixer future (unverified).
  One guide says **founding an Office costs 5,000,000 Ahn** and lets you pick any affiliation.
- **Grade exams:**
  - **Trivia** at Grade 9.
  - **Physical Competence** against a **Hana Association Examiner** at Grades 8 to 5: knock it down
    to about 70-60% HP, or at Grade 5 survive 45 seconds untouched.
  - **Obstacle Course** at Grades 4 to 2. Grade 2 adds a turret you must block or parry, which
    knocks you back.
  - XP requirements rise, roughly 625 XP at Grade 9 up to 5,000 at Grade 2 in one guide's table, and
    **extra XP is lost on promotion**. Grade 1 opens the path to the **Hana Association** after much
    more XP.
- **Combat:** light attack, block, dodge, **parry** and a **Critical** button. Skills are **Pages**:
  up to **5 combat Pages** equipped, not counting E.G.O and Singularity skills, and an NPC resets
  them. There are also passives and traits.
- **Builds: Sin Affinity trees.** Seven trees, with **Desire** standing in for Lust. You get **5
  points per Grade-up, max 45**, and each tree gives flat stats plus traits. A fan wiki lists an
  eighth, "Shin" (unverified).

| Tree | Status theme | Example scaling (guides) |
|---|---|---|
| Wrath | Burn | More damage the more health you have lost |
| Desire (Lust) | Bleed | Extra status applications at 15 and 30 points |
| Sloth | Tremor | Slower movement, more damage; stamina, block, parry and guard-break perks |
| Gluttony | Rupture | Natural health regeneration |
| Gloom | Sinking | E.G.O duration, about 3 times the Gloom points |
| Pride | Poise | Clash power |
| Envy | Charge | Extra positive status effects |

- **E.G.O manifestation:** requires Grade 3+ and enough "E.G.O experience", earned from consuming
  Seed of Light items, burning pages, PvP kills and quests. On success you revive and manifest your
  E.G.O permanently.
- **Rolls and surnames:** I found **no documented surname or lineage roll** in Archived. Its main
  "roll" is the affiliation that character creation produces. A rollable-surname system would be
  your own design. Roblox RPGs commonly use clan or lineage spins with tiered odds; if you sell
  spins, see 12.3.

### 11.4 What to borrow for Undermere

- **Grade exams as milestone events:** a trivia card at Grade 9, a clash gauntlet for Grades 8 to 5,
  a timed survival or "parry the turret" card for Grades 4 to 2. Hana is the examiner.
- **Dialogue-built origins:** childhood choices that set a starting affiliation fit a BitLife-style
  life well.
- **Sin-affinity build points** earned per promotion, each tied to one status effect (7.5) and one
  of the kit's stats.
- **A five-Page loadout** as the "approaches" or skills a character brings to clashes.
- **Office founding** as a late-game money sink that unlocks a new career branch.
- **Invitation-only group dungeons** (the Library) for crews.
- **E.G.O manifestation at the breaking point.** The kit already has one: at Sanity -45 a character
  either "goes under" or "Surfaces". That matches canon well: Distortion versus Effloresced E.G.O.

## 12. Roblox safety notes

### 12.1 Platform rules that shape this setting

- **Content maturity labels**, set by the creator:
  - **Minimal:** occasional mild violence, light unrealistic blood, occasional mild fear.
  - **Mild:** repeated mild violence, heavier unrealistic blood, mild crude humor, repeated mild fear.
  - **Moderate:** moderate violence, light realistic blood, moderate fear, "unplayable" gambling
    content.
  - **Restricted:** 18+ and age-checked. Per an ESRB summary it covers strong violence, heavy
    realistic blood, romantic themes, the presence of alcohol and strong language.
  - To reach the widest audience, target **Minimal or Mild**.
- **Off-limits regardless:**
  - Realistic extreme gore and graphic violence or death.
  - Showing, describing methods of, or glorifying **self-harm or suicide**, or presenting despair as
    desirable.
  - **Controlled substances**, which Roblox's rules say include alcohol, tobacco and vaping, plus
    paraphernalia and intoxicated behavior.
  - Sexual content.
  - Note on alcohol: the label system puts it under Restricted, while the Rules of Conduct list it
    among banned substances. Either way, keep it out of an all-ages game.
- **The house style that follows:** violence is implied, not shown. A fight is a clash card and a
  result. Death happens offscreen. Monsters are eerie, not anatomical.

### 12.2 Dark canon elements and how to present them

| Canon element | What canon has | Safe approach |
|---|---|---|
| **District 23's food culture** | Openly practiced cannibalism; restaurants such as Pierre's Bistro | **Leave the cannibalism out.** Keep "the Streets of Flavor" as the City's most cutthroat food scene: cooking duels, secret recipes, purple salt, restaurant feuds. Never imply what is in the meat. Replace Pierre's Bistro with an original rival |
| **The Thumb's punishments** | Maiming people over disrespect | "Disciplined" offscreen: fines, demotion, groveling apologies, or an injury condition such as a broken arm. No mutilation |
| **The Smoke War** | Atrocities, insect bio-weapons, child test subjects | Tell it as history and memory: veterans' stigma, propaganda posters, rumor-weapons seen as silhouettes. "Drafted young" instead of child experiments |
| **Body-horror Abnormalities** | Nothing There, CENSORED, Mountain of Smiling Bodies, Melting Love, Flesh Idol, Nameless Fetus, Singing Machine, Spider Bud, Meat Lantern, Queen Bee's spores | **Leave out** CENSORED, Mountain of Smiling Bodies, Flesh Idol, Nameless Fetus and Singing Machine. Show the rest only by effect: a dark unit, a borrowed voice, odd sounds. Prefer the gentler roster in 5.9 |
| **The Sin "Lust"** | One of the seven affinities | Rename it **Desire** (Archived does) or **Longing**. Keep it orange and keep the mechanics. No sexual framing |
| **Enkephalin and Canned Experience** | An addictive, hallucinogenic substance handed out to staff; memories eaten from cans | Enkephalin is **fuel and power cells only**: no consumption, no addiction stories. Canned Experience becomes **memory reels you watch**. Leave Porccubus out |
| **Smoking** | Ryōshū's chain-smoking; "one last cigarette" lines | Remove. Give her a sketchbook, brush or toothpick |
| **Gambling** | Rodion's betting; J Corp's casino world | No betting with any currency. A casino can be an atmospheric location with no playable gambling. Coin-flip clashes are combat RNG, which is fine |
| **Alcohol** | Pubs, toasts to the fallen | Teahouses and cafés; toast with tea. No intoxication |
| **Self-harm and suicide** | Lab deaths, a LobCorp mechanic in which clerks take their own lives, Carmen's death | **Never depict or mention methods.** Lives end offscreen ("was lost"). Frame "going under" at Sanity -45 as a transformation or loss, never a choice to die |
| **Gore and dissolving** | Sweepers melting bodies; Bloodfiends; the Ring's "art"; WARP passengers deformed | Sweepers "sweep away" with a sound and empty streets at dawn. Bloodfiends become "Night-blooded" who need a red tonic, with no drinking from people. The Ring makes eerie mannequins and installations. WARP riders "come back changed" |
| **Distortions** | Grotesque transformations | Stylized monsters made of light, shadow and objects; describe mood, not flesh |
| **Assassination work** | Shi contracts, Fixer hits | "Takedown", "retrieval", "neutralize the threat", resolved by a clash. No targeting of civilians |
| **Bondage and slavery terms** | S Corp's Nobi and slave-hunters, J Corp's miner slaves, Heishou "leashes" | Use "bonded", "debt-bound" or "contract-bound". Freedom arcs are fine. Avoid real-world historical slavery framing |
| **Human experiments** | The L Corp lab, G Corp test subjects, R Corp clones | "Risky trials", "volunteers", "hatched soldiers". No torture |
| **Mass erasures and reprisals** | Taboo erasures, Syndicate massacres | "Vanished overnight", "the block emptied". No massacre scenes |
| **Romance** | Wuthering Heights and similar | Keep bonds as family or friendship. Restricted covers romantic themes |
| **K Corp's secret** | A crying creature forced to watch violent footage | Keep the moral mystery, described as "a sorrowful creature kept watching sad reels". No violent footage |
| **H Corp body modification** | Grafted creature parts | Cosmetic animal traits (ears, tails, scales) with no surgery |
| **Mephistopheles' Enkephalin device** | It turns people into fuel | Leave out |
| **Prescripts ordering harm** | Index orders that can demand killings | Use odd, harmless or ambiguous Prescripts only |
| **Ricardo** | Sadistic violence | Keep the menace and the comedy; violence offscreen |

### 12.3 Rolls, surnames and money

- If surname, lineage or affiliation **rolls can be bought with Robux**, or with currency bought
  with Robux, they are **paid random items** under Roblox policy:
  - Show every outcome and its odds before purchase.
  - Honor `PolicyService` restrictions (`ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted`) for players where they apply.
  - Do not let ineligible players trade paid random outcomes.
- The simplest compliant design is free rolls earned in play, with no paid rerolls.
- Keep the kit's current rule ("looks only, never pay-to-win") for anything rollable that affects
  stats.
